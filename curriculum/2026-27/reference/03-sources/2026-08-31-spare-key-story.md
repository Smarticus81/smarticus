# The Spare Key
Grade 6 original fiction source for Monday, August 31, 2026.

By the time Malik reached the makerspace, the rain had already turned the windows gray. His team had one hour left to test the small weather station they had built for the school courtyard.

The station was supposed to record temperature and rainfall. On Friday, Malik had volunteered to carry the sensor case home so he could tighten a loose bracket. He had also promised to bring it back first thing Monday.

When he opened his backpack, the case was there. The tiny brass key that opened it was not.

Malik checked every pocket twice. Across the room, Priya was connecting wires while Jonah loaded the data program. “We should start the sensor test,” Priya said. Malik felt his face grow warm.

He could tell them the lock had jammed. That would buy time. Maybe the key was in his room, under a notebook or beside the charger. If he stayed quiet, no one would know he had misplaced it.

Then Jonah held up the test checklist. “Step one says inspect the sensor before power-on. We can’t do that with the case locked.”

Malik looked at the case and then at the clock. “I lost the key,” he said. “I think it’s at home. I should’ve checked before I left.”

No one cheered. Priya closed her eyes for a second. Then she opened the supply cabinet. “There might be a spare.”

They searched through labeled drawers until Jonah found a small envelope taped behind the cabinet door: WEATHER STATION — SPARE KEY. The test began twenty minutes late.

During the first trial, the rainfall sensor gave impossible numbers. Because the case was open, Malik noticed that one wire had loosened during the trip home. He reconnected it, and the second trial worked.

At cleanup, Malik wrote a new line on the checklist: RETURN KEY TO LAB BEFORE TAKING CASE HOME. Priya added another: CHECK SPARE BEFORE TEST DAY.

“So we’re making a rule because you lost a key?” Jonah asked.

Malik shook his head. “We’re making a better system because one person shouldn’t be able to stop the whole test.”

Priya nodded. “That I can use.”
