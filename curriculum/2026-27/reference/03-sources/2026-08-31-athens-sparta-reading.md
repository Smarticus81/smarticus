# Athens and Sparta — Two Poleis, Different Priorities
Grade 6 history source for Monday, August 31, 2026.

Athens and Sparta were two influential Greek poleis. Both spoke Greek, worshiped many of the same gods, and participated in shared festivals, but they organized society differently.

Athens developed near the sea and became active in trade. Public speaking, law, drama, philosophy, and civic debate became important parts of Athenian life. Adult male citizens could participate directly in parts of government. This system is often called an early democracy, although women, enslaved people, and many other residents did not have full political rights.

Sparta developed a society centered more heavily on military readiness and discipline. A small group of Spartan citizens trained for warfare, while other groups performed much of the farming and labor. Sparta had kings, councils, and assemblies, but political power was concentrated in fewer hands than in Athens.

The contrast shows why the Greek world was not one uniform society. Poleis shared cultural traditions while making different choices about government, education, military life, and public participation.
