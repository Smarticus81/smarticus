# Grade 6 Mathematics — Syllabus + Teaching Guide

## Course purpose
Build secure Grade 6 number sense, proportional reasoning, algebraic thinking, geometry, and statistics while making reasoning visible. Do not skip prerequisite instruction simply because a topic belongs to Grade 6.

## Standards reference
Primary organizing reference: Common Core Grade 6 Mathematics domains 6.RP, 6.NS, 6.EE, 6.G, and 6.SP. Texas Grade 6 expectations should be considered when selecting examples and ensuring transferability.

## Year sequence
### Unit 1 — Number Operations and Fluency
- Fraction multiplication/division review and application
- Decimal multiplication/division
- Multi-digit computation and estimation
- GCF/LCM and distributive reasoning as needed

### Unit 2 — Ratios and Rates
- Ratio language and representations
- Equivalent ratios and tables
- Unit rates
- Double number lines / coordinate representations

### Unit 3 — Percent
- Percent as rate per 100
- Fraction/decimal/percent connections
- Percent of a quantity
- Finding whole/part/percent in Grade 6 contexts

### Unit 4 — Rational Numbers
- Positive/negative numbers in context
- Number line and absolute value
- Ordering rational numbers
- Coordinate plane in all quadrants

### Unit 5 — Expressions
- Exponents and order of operations
- Variables and expressions
- Equivalent expressions and distributive property
- Evaluating expressions

### Unit 6 — Equations and Inequalities
- One-step and simple multi-step equations appropriate to Grade 6
- Meaning of equality
- Inequalities and solution sets
- Real-world modeling

### Unit 7 — Geometry
- Area of triangles/quadrilaterals/polygons
- Volume of rectangular prisms
- Surface area and nets
- Coordinate geometry applications

### Unit 8 — Statistics
- Statistical questions
- Dot plots, histograms, box plots as appropriate
- Mean/median and variability
- Describe distributions in context

## Teaching routine
For every new procedure:
1. Explain the underlying quantity/place-value/fraction meaning.
2. Model one simple example.
3. Model one example with an additional step.
4. Model one application/reasonableness check.
5. Guided practice with hints.
6. Independent practice.
7. Exit ticket requiring both calculation and explanation when appropriate.

## Current opening-unit guide
### Fraction multiplication
Key idea: multiply numerators and denominators, but convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first. Simplify before or after multiplication when valid.
Common misconceptions:
- Multiplying whole parts separately from fractional parts.
- Cross-canceling numbers that are not numerator/denominator factors across multiplication.
- Forgetting to convert mixed numbers.

Model sequence:
1. 3/5 × 4/7 = 12/35.
2. 5/6 × 9/10: simplify 5 with 10 and 9 with 6, then multiply = 3/4.
3. 1 1/2 × 2/3 = 3/2 × 2/3 = 1.

### Fraction division
Key idea: division asks how many groups of the divisor fit into the dividend. The reciprocal rule transforms division into equivalent multiplication.
Procedure: keep the dividend, change division to multiplication, flip only the divisor, then simplify/multiply.
Common misconceptions:
- Flipping both fractions.
- Flipping the dividend.
- Treating division like multiplication without using the reciprocal.
- Converting mixed numbers incorrectly.

Model sequence:
1. 2/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2/3 × 6/1 = 4.
2. 5/8 ÷ 3/4 = 5/8 × 4/3 = 5/6.
3. 1 1/2 ÷ 3/5 = 3/2 × 5/3 = 5/2 = 2 1/2.

Current evidence: fraction division requires reinforcement before treating it as secure.

### Decimal multiplication
Teach through place value, not only a decimal-counting trick.
Example: 12.6 × 0.4. Estimate first (~5). Compute 126 × 4 = 504 hundredths = 5.04.
Example: 3.25 × 1.6 = 5.20 = 5.2.

### Decimal division
Explain why multiplying dividend and divisor by the same power of ten preserves the quotient.
Example: 8.64 ÷ 0.27 = 864 ÷ 27 = 32.
Do not say only "move the decimal" without the equivalence explanation.

## Reasonableness checks
Use estimates whenever decimal placement or operation choice could produce implausible answers. Ask: should the result be greater or less than the starting quantity? Approximately how large?

## Mastery evidence
A topic is ready for faster pacing when Atticus can:
- solve representative Grade 6 items accurately;
- show understandable work;
- explain why the procedure works at an age-appropriate level;
- solve at least one unfamiliar application;
- repeat mastery on more than one occasion.

## Feedback language
Name the step that broke down. Example: "Your conversion to 5/2 is correct. The next step is to flip only 5/8 because it is the divisor." Avoid saying merely "wrong."
