# Atticus Grade 6 Master Glossary

Purpose: give the voice tutor and daily packets a shared, student-friendly definition source. Definitions should be explained in plain Grade 6 language first, then used accurately in academic context. If Atticus appears unsure of a term, define it immediately, give one example, and return to the lesson.

## Mathematics
- quotient: the answer to a division problem.
- dividend: the number being divided.
- divisor: the number you divide by.
- reciprocal: a fraction made by switching numerator and denominator; the reciprocal of 3/5 is 5/3.
- equivalent: equal in value even if written differently.
- estimate: a close prediction used to check whether an exact answer makes sense.
- magnitude: the size of a number or quantity.
- product: the answer to a multiplication problem.

## Literature / Reading
- topic: the specific subject a text is about.
- central idea: the main point an author communicates about a topic.
- theme: a complete message about life, people, or human experience developed by a literary text.
- evidence: a specific detail, fact, example, or quotation that supports an idea.
- inference: a conclusion made from evidence plus reasoning rather than directly stated information.
- significance: why a detail matters or what it shows.
- objective summary: a short account of the most important ideas without personal opinion.
- context clue: information around an unfamiliar word that helps determine its meaning.

## Writing
- claim: the main point or position being supported.
- evidence: a fact or detail used to support a claim or explanation.
- reasoning: the explanation of why the evidence supports the main idea or claim.
- explanatory writing: writing that teaches how or why something happens.
- topic sentence: the sentence that states the main idea of a paragraph.
- transition: a word or phrase that helps ideas connect.
- revise: to improve ideas, organization, wording, or sentence clarity.
- edit: to correct conventions such as spelling, punctuation, capitalization, and grammar.

## Science
- transmit: to let light pass through.
- reflect: to bounce light away from a surface.
- absorb: to take in light energy rather than transmit or reflect it.
- scatter: to redirect light in many directions.
- transparent: transmits most visible light with little scattering, allowing a clear image.
- translucent: transmits some visible light but scatters it, so images are blurry or unclear.
- opaque: does not transmit visible light through the material.
- model: a representation used to explain or predict how a system works.
- observation: information recorded from what is measured or directly noticed.
- hypothesis: a testable proposed explanation or prediction.
- thermal expansion: the increase in size of many materials as they warm.
- contract: to become smaller.
- stress: internal force in a material caused by pushing, pulling, bending, or blocked movement.

## History / Geography / Civics
- polis: an independent Greek city-state and its surrounding territory/community.
- city-state: a city and nearby territory functioning as an independent political unit.
- politically independent: governing itself rather than being ruled by one larger central government.
- culturally connected: sharing language, beliefs, customs, stories, or practices even without one government.
- harbor: a sheltered place where ships can anchor safely.
- trade: exchange of goods, resources, or services.
- geography: the study of places, environments, and how people interact with them.
- cause and effect: a relationship in which one event or condition helps produce another result.

## French
- subject pronoun: a word such as je, tu, il, elle, nous, vous, ils, or elles that tells who does the action.
- stem: the part of a regular verb left after removing -er.
- ending: letters added to a verb stem to match the subject.
- conjugate: to change a verb form to match the subject.
- negation: a form that makes a sentence negative, such as ne...pas.
- elision: dropping a vowel before another vowel sound; for example, ne becomes n'.
- affirmative: a sentence that states something positively rather than negatively.

## Computer Science / AI
- algorithm: a precise ordered set of steps for solving a problem or completing a task.
- conditional: a decision that makes a program choose an action based on whether something is true or false.
- IF/THEN/ELSE: a common conditional structure: if a condition is true, do one action; otherwise do another.
- rule-based program: a program that follows explicit instructions written by a programmer.
- machine learning: a method in which a system learns patterns from examples rather than receiving every decision rule directly.
- training data: examples used to teach a machine-learning model.
- label: the correct category name attached to a training example.
- classifier: a model that assigns an input to one of several categories.
- confidence: the model's numerical estimate of how strongly an input matches a class; it is not a guarantee of correctness.
- generalize: to work correctly on new examples or conditions not seen exactly during training.
- debugging: finding, understanding, and fixing a problem in a program or system.

## Art / Design
- visual hierarchy: the order in which a viewer notices information.
- contrast: a noticeable difference that helps important elements stand out.
- symbol: a simple image or mark that represents an idea.
- annotation: a short label or note added to explain part of a visual.
- composition: the arrangement of elements on a page or image.
- caption: brief text that explains an image or diagram.
