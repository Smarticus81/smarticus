# Grade 6 Science — Syllabus + Teaching Guide

## Purpose
Develop scientific understanding through phenomena, models, investigations, evidence, and explanation rather than vocabulary memorization alone.

## Standards reference
Primary organizing reference: NGSS middle-school performance expectations and science/engineering practices. The sequence follows an OpenSciEd-style middle-school progression.

## Year sequence
1. Light and Matter
2. Thermal Energy
3. Weather, Climate, and Water Cycling
4. Plate Tectonics and Rock Cycling
5. Natural Hazards
6. Cells and Systems

Practices throughout: asking questions, modeling, planning/performing investigations, measuring, analyzing data, constructing explanations, arguing from evidence, communicating results.

## Current unit — Light and Matter
Core question: How does light interact with matter, and how does light allow us to see?

### Essential ideas
- Light travels from a source and can interact with materials.
- Materials may transmit, reflect, absorb, or scatter light to different degrees.
- We see an object when visible light from the object or reflected from it enters our eyes.
- A non-luminous object such as a book can be seen because light from another source reflects from the book into the eye.
- Mirrors produce relatively directional reflection; ordinary surfaces such as white paper can also reflect light diffusely.
- Transparent, translucent, and opaque describe how materials transmit visible light, but real materials can show more than one interaction.

### Model examples
Lamp: source → eye.
Book: lamp/source → book → eye.
Mirror: source → mirror → eye/wall, with strong directional reflection.
Clear plastic: much light transmits through; some reflection may still occur.

## Investigation routine
1. State the question.
2. Predict and explain why.
3. Keep relevant conditions consistent.
4. Observe before interpreting.
5. Record actual evidence, including unexpected results.
6. Build/revise a model.
7. Write a CER from the evidence.

## Observation vs inference
Observation: "A bright spot appeared on the wall after light hit the foil."
Inference/explanation: "The foil reflected a substantial amount of light."
Do not merge these too early.

## CER rubric — 12 points
Claim 0–3: directly answers the question and is scientifically defensible.
Evidence 0–3: specific observations/data from the investigation.
Reasoning 0–4: explains how evidence supports the claim using the science idea.
Communication 0–2: complete, clear, appropriate vocabulary/diagram labels.

## Current calibration
Opening-week work shows meaningful understanding of reflection and how reflected light enables vision. Continue improving evidence precision. White paper should not be treated simply as an absorber; it visibly reflects/scatters light diffusely even though it is not mirror-like.

## Teaching rules
Do not announce the expected experimental result before observation. If Atticus's result differs from the model, first examine setup/evidence rather than telling him to change it. Use diagrams and ray/path models whenever they clarify mechanisms.
