# Atticus Grade 6 Master Curriculum — 2026–27

## Purpose
This document is the canonical academic map for Atticus's 2026–27 Grade 6 homeschool year. Daily lessons must fit inside this map. The voice tutor uses it to explain where today's lesson sits in the larger course, what prerequisites matter, what comes next, and when acceleration is appropriate.

This is a parent-directed Texas homeschool program. It is designed to be measurable, standards-referenced, portfolio-ready, and understandable to an outside school. It is not represented as an accredited school transcript, and a receiving institution retains authority over placement or transfer decisions.

## Governing instructional rules
1. Teach before testing. Never assume a Grade 6 concept has already been taught.
2. For every new concept, provide clear explanation plus 2–3 complete examples/models before independent work.
3. Use Grade 6 material by default.
4. Acceleration occurs only when current Grade 6 work shows repeated mastery and the material is becoming too easy.
5. A student saying "I have not learned this" is diagnostic information, not failure.
6. Accuracy, reasoning, completion, revision, and explanation matter more than speed.
7. Every lesson should connect to prior learning and to the next planned skill.
8. Themes should vary naturally; do not default to soccer.
9. Independent assessment answers are protected. Hints and analogous examples come before full solutions.
10. Student-facing feedback must be specific, evidence-based, and actionable.

## Mastery model
- 90%+ on two meaningful demonstrations, including at least one explanation or unfamiliar application: eligible for faster pacing or greater complexity.
- 75–89%: continue Grade 6 sequence with reinforcement.
- Below 75% or a major conceptual misconception: reteach before advancing.
- A numeric score alone does not override evidence of a foundational gap.

## Courses

### Grade 6 Mathematics
Primary standards reference: Common Core Grade 6 Mathematics, with Texas Grade 6 mathematics alignment considered when planning.
Sequence:
1. Number operations review and fraction/decimal fluency
2. Ratios and equivalent ratios
3. Unit rates and proportional reasoning foundations
4. Percent as rate per 100 and percent applications
5. Rational numbers and the coordinate plane
6. Numerical and algebraic expressions
7. One-variable equations and inequalities
8. Geometry: area, surface area, volume, coordinate applications
9. Statistics: distributions, center, variability, data displays
10. Cumulative problem solving and readiness assessment

### Grade 6 Literature / Reading
Primary standards reference: Common Core Grade 6 Reading Literature and Reading Informational Text.
Sequence:
1. Close reading, inference, evidence, theme, central idea, summary
2. Character, plot, point of view, structure
3. Informational text: claims, evidence, cause/effect, structure
4. Novel study and sustained reading
5. Comparing texts and perspectives
6. Research reading and source evaluation
7. Cumulative literary/informational analysis

### Grade 6 Writing / Language
Primary standards reference: Common Core Grade 6 Writing and Language.
Sequence:
1. Complete responses, CER, paragraph structure, revision habits
2. Explanatory writing
3. Narrative writing
4. Argument writing
5. Research and source integration
6. Grammar, sentence structure, punctuation, usage, vocabulary throughout
7. Multi-paragraph essays and cumulative writing portfolio

### Grade 6 Science
Primary standards reference: NGSS middle-school performance expectations, using an OpenSciEd-style phenomenon/model/investigation approach.
Sequence:
1. Light and Matter
2. Thermal Energy
3. Weather, Climate, and Water Cycling
4. Plate Tectonics and Rock Cycling
5. Natural Hazards
6. Cells and Systems
Scientific practices throughout: modeling, measurement, data, investigation design, claim-evidence-reasoning, revision from evidence.

### Grade 6 History / Geography / Civics
Primary references: Core Knowledge Grade 6 History & Geography sequence plus C3-style inquiry and evidence practices.
Sequence:
1. Geographic reasoning and map skills
2. Ancient Greece
3. Ancient Rome
4. Enlightenment
5. French Revolution
6. Industrial Revolution
7. Latin American independence
8. Immigration, industrialization, and reform
9. Civics/current-events inquiry and source evaluation throughout

### Grade 6 French
Primary reference: ACTFL World-Readiness communication goals. This is continuing French, not beginner French.
Sequence:
1. Present-tense consolidation: regular -ER verbs, negation, question forms
2. High-frequency irregular verbs: être, avoir, aller, faire
3. Description, family, routines, preferences
4. Food, places, travel, time, weather
5. Listening and short conversation
6. Reading short authentic/teacher-created texts
7. Guided paragraph writing and short oral presentations

### Grade 6 Computer Science / AI
Primary reference: CSTA middle-school concepts, with age-appropriate AI literacy.
Sequence:
1. Computational thinking, algorithms, sequencing, debugging
2. Conditionals, events, loops, variables
3. Web/programming foundations
4. Data representation and visualization
5. AI/ML concepts: examples, labels, training data, classification, limitations, bias
6. Online build projects using browser-safe tools such as Scratch and Teachable Machine when appropriate
7. Capstone: design, build, test, explain

### Art / Design
Primary reference: National Core Arts Standards-style create/present/respond/connect goals.
Focus: drawing, observation, graphic design, photography, architecture/product design, visual communication, critique, portfolio pieces.

### PE / Wellness
Primary reference: middle-school physical-literacy goals. Scheduled when included in a day's program; not required in every packet. Focus on safe movement, fitness, coordination, mobility, and measurable personal improvement rather than a single sport.

## Assessment evidence
The program should preserve:
- Daily independent work selectively
- Exit tickets
- Unit quizzes and assessments
- Writing drafts and final versions
- Science lab records and CERs
- Reading responses and book log
- French oral/written evidence
- Computer science/AI projects
- Selected art/design portfolio work
- Quarterly narrative summaries

## Outside-review package
At any transfer/admissions point, the program should be able to produce course descriptions, scope and sequence, standards references, dated work samples, assessments, grades/mastery evidence, reading list, projects, attendance if desired, and parent/teacher narrative.

## Source priority
Today's assigned lesson controls today's work. The subject syllabus controls the planned course sequence. Unit/subject reference guidance controls explanations and prerequisites. Teacher feedback and current mastery evidence control pacing. General model knowledge may clarify facts, but it must not silently rewrite the planned course sequence.
