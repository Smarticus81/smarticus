# Grade 6 History / Geography / Civics — Syllabus + Teaching Guide

## Purpose
Build chronological, geographic, civic, and evidence-based reasoning. History should be taught as connected causes, choices, institutions, environments, and consequences—not isolated fact memorization.

## Course references
Core Knowledge Grade 6 History & Geography sequence is the main content spine. C3-style inquiry practices guide questioning, source use, evidence, and civic reasoning.

## Current unit — Ancient Greece
### Central question
How did geography, institutions, conflict, and ideas shape Greek civilization and its influence?

### Opening geography
Locate and explain the significance of:
- Mediterranean Sea
- Aegean Sea
- Balkan Peninsula / Greek mainland
- Crete
- Asia Minor / western Anatolia
- mountainous terrain
- islands and coastlines
- limited arable land in many areas

### Cause/effect chains
Mountains → difficult overland travel → separated communities → stronger local independence / city-state development.
Sea access → travel, fishing, trade, exchange → connections across the Aegean and Mediterranean.
Limited farmland → pressure for trade and settlement/colonization beyond the mainland.

## Historical reasoning habits
Before answering:
1. Place the topic on a map and timeline.
2. Identify what came before.
3. Distinguish cause from correlation.
4. Use evidence from the source/map/text.
5. Consider limits: who participated, who was excluded, what changed over time?

## Civics / good-citizenship strand
Practice respectful discussion, source evaluation, rights/responsibilities, institutions, rule of law, civic participation, and comparison of historical political systems with modern democratic principles. Avoid treating ancient Athens as equivalent to modern universal democracy; discuss who could and could not participate.

## Response rubric
Strong response: answers the causal question, names specific geographic/historical evidence, and explains the connection.
Weak response: lists facts without explaining why they matter.

## Teaching rules
Teach map/timeline context before deep detail. Use multiple causes when appropriate. Make clear when a statement is directly supported by a source versus an inference. Do not force modern moral categories onto ancient contexts without explanation, but do examine limits and consequences honestly.
