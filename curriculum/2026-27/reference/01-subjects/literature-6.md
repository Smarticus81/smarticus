# Grade 6 Literature / Reading — Syllabus + Teaching Guide

## Course purpose
Develop careful, evidence-based reading across fiction, nonfiction, and longer works. Atticus should learn to slow down, distinguish what a text literally says from what it implies, and turn understanding into complete academic responses.

## Standards reference
Primary organizing reference: Common Core Grade 6 Reading Literature (RL.6) and Reading Informational Text (RI.6), especially evidence, central ideas/themes, summary, vocabulary in context, structure, point of view, argument/evidence, and comparing sources.

## Year sequence
1. Close reading foundations: evidence, inference, vocabulary in context, theme, central idea, objective summary
2. Character development, conflict, plot, setting, point of view
3. Informational text: central idea, cause/effect, structure, evidence
4. Novel study and sustained reading
5. Comparing texts, perspectives, and media
6. Research reading and source quality
7. Cumulative analysis and portfolio

## Core response habits
Before writing, identify every part of the question. If the prompt says "give two effects," the answer must contain two effects. If it asks for evidence, the response must include a specific detail. If it asks significance, explain what the detail reveals/proves.

## Key distinctions
### Topic vs theme
Topic: one or two words such as failure, teamwork, courage.
Theme: a complete message about people/life, such as "People improve when they study mistakes instead of hiding them."

### Topic vs central idea
Topic: what the text is about.
Central idea: the most important point the author communicates about that topic.

### Summary vs analysis
Summary tells the important events/ideas objectively. Analysis explains how/why a detail, structure, choice, or idea matters.

### Literal meaning vs significance
Literal: what happened.
Significance: what that event/detail proves, changes, or reveals.

## Worked response models
### Vocabulary in context
Sentence context shows a team testing one variable at a time and recording results. "Methodical" therefore means systematic, careful, and organized—not simply "using different methods."

### Theme
Weak: "Figuring things out."
Strong: "People solve difficult problems more effectively when they test ideas carefully and learn from mistakes."

### Evidence response
Claim: The character becomes more collaborative.
Evidence: Early he rejects teammates' suggestions; later he asks to test their ideas.
Explanation: The contrast shows he has changed from valuing only his own first idea to valuing evidence and teamwork.

## Current calibration
Opening-week work shows solid literal comprehension and useful inference. Academic-response precision needs continued work: complete theme/central-idea statements, evidence selection, significance, and full completion of multi-part prompts.

## Reading routine
1. First read: overall meaning.
2. Second read: annotate key evidence, unfamiliar language, changes/cause-effect.
3. State likely central idea/theme in a complete sentence.
4. Select the strongest supporting details.
5. Answer every part of the written question.
6. Re-read the question before submitting.

## Independent reading
Maintain regular sustained reading. Track title, author, dates, pages/chapters, short reaction, and occasional discussion/response. Quality and comprehension matter more than inflated book counts.

### AR-weighted reading goals for 2026-27
Use official Renaissance Accelerated Reader maximum point values as **book weights** in the homeschool tracker. This homeschool tracking system is separate from Renaissance's official quiz-scoring system.

Daily routine:
- Read at least 25 minutes on school days.
- If an unfamiliar word does not block the sentence, mark it and keep reading.
- If the word blocks meaning, ask Virgil for a one-sentence plain-English definition, reread the sentence, and continue.
- End with one sentence: **What changed or became clearer today?**

To bank a book's full weight:
1. Finish the book.
2. Give a 60-second oral summary without notes.
3. Score 80% or better on a fresh homeschool comprehension check.
4. Answer one deeper question about theme, character, author choice, or evidence.
5. If below 80%, review weak areas and complete a new check.

## Mastery indicators
- Cites appropriate textual evidence.
- Identifies theme/central idea as a complete message.
- Produces objective summaries without unnecessary opinion.
- Uses context to determine meaning.
- Explains significance, not just plot.
- Completes every part of a prompt.
- Sustains independent reading and demonstrates comprehension across longer works.
