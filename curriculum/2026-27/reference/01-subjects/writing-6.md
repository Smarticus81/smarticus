# Grade 6 Writing / Language — Syllabus + Teaching Guide

## Purpose
Turn strong ideas into complete, organized, evidence-based writing. Writing is taught as a process: understand the task, plan, model, draft, revise, edit, submit.

## Standards reference
Primary organizing reference: Common Core Grade 6 Writing (W.6) and Language (L.6): argument, informative/explanatory writing, narrative writing, research, revision, conventions, vocabulary.

## Year sequence
1. Complete academic responses and CER
2. Evidence-based explanatory paragraphs
3. Multi-paragraph explanatory essays
4. Narrative craft and structure
5. Argument writing with claims/evidence
6. Research, paraphrase, source integration
7. Revision, style, grammar, vocabulary throughout
8. Final portfolio and timed/on-demand writing

## Core structure: evidence-based explanation
Topic/claim → Evidence 1 → Reasoning 1 → Evidence 2 → Reasoning 2 → Conclusion.
Evidence is the fact/detail. Reasoning explains why that evidence supports the claim.

## CER model
Claim: A clear answer to the exact question.
Evidence: Specific observation/detail/data/text evidence.
Reasoning: The logical bridge explaining why the evidence supports the claim.

Weak reasoning: "This proves my claim."
Stronger reasoning: "Because the foil reflected the beam strongly toward the wall while little light passed through, the observation supports the claim that foil was a strong reflector."

## Current calibration
Opening work shows substantial ideas and willingness to write. Main priorities are organization, sentence boundaries, explicit reasoning, and final proofreading. Do not reduce the intellectual demand; provide structure and models.

## Revision checklist
- Did I answer the exact question?
- Did I complete every required part?
- Is the main idea/claim clear?
- Did I use the required evidence?
- Did I explain why the evidence matters?
- Are sentences complete and separated correctly?
- Did I check capitalization, punctuation, spelling, and obvious word choice?

## Rubric — 20 points
Content/claim: 0–4
Evidence/details: 0–4
Reasoning/development: 0–4
Organization/coherence: 0–4
Conventions/revision: 0–4

A strong Grade 6 response is not defined by length alone. It must be complete, organized, supported, and revised.

## Teaching rules
Always show 2–3 models before a new writing form. Include at least one weak-vs-strong comparison when helpful. Do not write Atticus's final paragraph for him. Help him orally plan the components, then require his own wording.
